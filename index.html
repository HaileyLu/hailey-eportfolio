What my placement taught me

My experience at Sabre Medical shaped how I understand quality, collaboration and responsibility in biomedical engineering.

Expectations and reality

Before beginning my internship, I expected that my role would primarily involve applying the technical knowledge I had gained at university. I imagined that engineering work would mainly consist of designing products, solving technical problems and developing new technologies. My experience introduced me to the importance of verification and validation, quality systems, documentation and regulatory requirements in ensuring that medical devices are safe and effective.

As a Student Engineer within the New Process Introduction Engineering team, I contributed to packaging validation, accelerated ageing studies, functional testing and transportation simulation. I also gained exposure to sterile barrier integrity testing and pouch sealing validation within a regulated manufacturing environment. One of the most significant differences between my expectations and reality was the level of detail and documentation required in industry. At university, engineering projects often focus on achieving a technical solution. My workplace experience showed me that it is equally important to demonstrate and document that a product consistently meets its requirements. Processes must be planned, tested and recorded because the quality and reliability of a medical device can directly affect patient safety.

Lessons and professional identity

One of the most important lessons I learned was the importance of quality and attention to detail. I developed a greater appreciation for the role of standards, testing procedures and documentation in engineering. This changed my understanding of what it means to be an engineer: creating solutions also involves responsibility for their safety, reliability and suitability for their intended purpose. I learned the importance of collaboration and communication. Working alongside engineers and other professionals showed me that successful projects require clear communication, established processes and a shared goal.

What I bring to an employer

Following this experience, I believe my value proposition is a combination of technical knowledge, industry experience and willingness to learn. I can bring an understanding of engineering principles and practical experience in a regulated medical device environment. I can demonstrate this through supporting verification and validation activities, conducting and assisting with testing processes, and working within established quality procedures. My internship strengthened my ability to adapt to a professional workplace, learn new processes and approach tasks with attention to detail.

Direction

Before my placement, I was primarily interested in the design and development side of biomedical engineering. My experience at Sabre Medical expanded my interests to include verification and validation, quality engineering and medical device development. I became interested in the process between an initial engineering concept and a final product that can safely be used in healthcare. I enjoy working on practical engineering problems while contributing to the safety, quality and reliability of medical technologies. I hope to pursue a career contributing to the development and improvement of medical devices that make a meaningful difference in people’s lives.
